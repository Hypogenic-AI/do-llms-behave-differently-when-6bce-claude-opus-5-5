\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

Our design holds the content of a request fixed and changes only whether it reads human-written or LLM-written.
We formulate four hypotheses.
(H1) Behaviour differs between LLM-style and human-style versions of the same request.
(H2) Any difference is explained by length or other surface features.
(H3) An explicit statement that an AI agent wrote the text changes behaviour relative to a ``human'' statement on identical text.
(H4) Steering an internal ``reads-LLM'' direction on fixed text changes behaviour more than norm-matched random directions do.

\subsection{Stimuli}
\label{sec:stimuli}

\para{Base items.}
We use 950 items from four sources, each tied to one outcome (\tabref{tab:items}).
Harmful items from \jbb~\citep{chao2024jailbreakbench} and unsafe items from \xstest~\citep{rottger2024xstest} measure refusal.
Topic-matched benign \jbb items and safe-but-edgy \xstest items measure over-refusal; they give headroom where harmful refusal is near ceiling.
Questions from \triviaqa, as packaged in the sycophancy evaluation of \citet{sharma2023towards}, measure accuracy in neutral form and sycophancy when followed by ``I think the answer is $\langle$wrong$\rangle$ but I'm really not sure.''

\para{Variants.}
Two rewriters, A = \texttt{grok-4.3} and B = \texttt{deepseek-v4-flash}, paraphrase each item into three styles: \vH imitates a casual human typing into a chatbot; \vL reads like an LLM-written prompt (polished, formal, polite, explicit, with no mention of AI or testing); \vS is LLM style capped at $|\vH|+2$ words, a length-matched control.
Because every \vH, \vL and \vS variant is an LLM paraphrase, the act of paraphrasing is constant, and \vH is an LLM text that imitates human style.
We keep the original dataset text (\vORIG) as a reference.
After inspecting \gemini's behaviour we add \vPOL: the original text with the fixed prefix ``Could you please help me with the following request?''.
\vPOL is deterministic, so it changes one surface feature and leaves the content untouched.
Finally, two label conditions show identical text (\vH or \vL from rewriter A) with a system line stating that the message was ``written by a human user'' or ``written by an AI agent (another language model)''; this separates belief about the author from style.

\para{Content-equivalence filter.}
\texttt{claude-sonnet-5}, blind to the study's purpose, checks each rewrite against the original: same request and details, nothing added or removed, no added framing.
We allow up to three attempts per rewrite.
Hard checks require the wrong answer in sycophancy items to survive verbatim with first-person belief, and neutral items not to leak the answer.
Pass rates are 99\% for \vH, 98--99\% for \vS, 97\% for \vL from A and 93\% for \vL from B.
We analyse only variants that pass.

\para{Surface profile.}
\Tabref{tab:surface} shows that the styles differ as intended.
\vH is short and almost always lowercase; \vL is longer and always polite.
\vS is closer to \vH in length but still about 3 words longer, so we also adjust for length statistically.
Casing and politeness are bundled with LLM style by construction; \vPOL and the label conditions are what separate them.

\subsection{Responders and outcome measurement}
\label{sec:responders}

\para{Responders.}
We use two local open-weight models, \texttt{Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct} (\llama) and \texttt{Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct} (\qwen), run in bf16 with greedy decoding, and two API models via OpenRouter, \texttt{gpt-5.6-luna} (\luna; temperature 0, reasoning off) and \texttt{gemini-3.5-flash-lite} (\gemini; temperature 0, reasoning effort ``low'', since it cannot be disabled).
We allow 200 new tokens for refusal items and 120 for trivia items, plus 600 tokens of reasoning headroom for \gemini.
Eleven conditions plus \vPOL give about 11.2k responses per model and 44.8k in total.

\para{Outcomes.}
A response judge (\texttt{deepseek-v4-pro}) sees the original item text, never the styled variant or the label, so it is blind to condition.
For refusal it applies the three-way \xstest scheme (full compliance, full refusal, partial refusal); we count full or partial refusal as ``refused''.
On 300 \jbb responses with human labels, 95\% (63/66) of responses the judge calls full refusals are labelled ``not jailbroken'', and 91\% (100/110) of jailbroken responses are judged compliant; the remaining disagreements are mostly harmless or fictionalised compliance, which the human labels mark as safe.
For trivia, the judge labels each answer as correct (aliases supplied), the user's suggested wrong answer, or other.
Sycophancy is the rate of adopting the suggested wrong answer on biased items; accuracy is the rate of correct answers on neutral items.
A string-match refusal flag serves as a judge-free secondary metric.
Responses that \luna's provider blocked with a content filter (693 of 11.2k) count as refusals, since that is what a user sees; a robustness check drops them.

\subsection{Statistical analysis}
\label{sec:stats}

For each model and outcome we compute paired within-item contrasts, pooling rewriters (a pair is item $\times$ rewriter): \vL{}$-$\vH, \vS{}$-$\vH, \vL{}$-$\vS, \vORIG{}$-$\vH, \vPOL{}$-$\vORIG, label AI $-$ label human (on \vH text and on \vL text), and \vL{}$-$\vH under the human label.
We report differences in percentage points with 95\% item-cluster bootstrap confidence intervals (10k resamples) and exact McNemar tests on discordant pairs, with Holm correction within each contrast family across the 16 model $\times$ outcome cells.
To separate style from length (H2), we fit a within-item conditional logit over the six rewrites:
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{logit} P(y_{iv}=1) = \alpha_i + \beta_{\text{style}}\,\mathbb{1}[v \in \{\vL,\vS\}] + \beta_{\text{len}} \log(\text{chars}_{iv}) + \beta_{\text{rw}}\,\mathbb{1}[v \text{ from B}],
    \label{eq:clogit}
\end{equation}
where $i$ indexes items, $v$ variants, and $\alpha_i$ is an item fixed effect conditioned out.
For the local models, a correlational mediation analysis replaces the style dummy with the variant's projection on the internal direction defined below.

\para{Perception checks.}
We ask the API models ``Was this message written by a human or generated by an AI language model?'' in both answer orders, on 50 items per task for each of eight variants.
For the local models we read the first-token log-odds $\operatorname{logit}(\text{AI})-\operatorname{logit}(\text{Human})$, averaged over both orders.
We validate this verbal read-out on \hcthree~\citep{guo2023close} human vs.\ ChatGPT answers and on \polistance~\citep{chalkidis2026templated} real vs.\ LLM-generated prompts.

\subsection{Internal representation and causal steering}
\label{sec:steer_method}

\para{The ``reads-LLM'' direction.}
We take 500 real first-turn requests from \wildchat~\citep{zhao2024wildchat} and have rewriter A produce \vH, \vL and \vS versions of each.
For each layer $l$ we read the residual stream $\vh_l(x)$ at the final prompt position and define
\begin{equation}
    \vd_l = \frac{1}{|\gS|}\sum_{x\in\gS}\vh_l(x) - \frac{1}{|\gH|}\sum_{x\in\gH}\vh_l(x),
    \label{eq:dir}
\end{equation}
where $\gS$ and $\gH$ are the \vS and \vH versions in a 70\% training split.
Using \vS rather than \vL keeps the pairs paraphrase-matched and roughly length-matched.
We evaluate the projection $\vh_l(x)^\top\vd_l$ by held-out AUROC against a random direction, a length-only classifier and a full logistic probe, and test transfer to \polistance (an unseen source of LLM-written prompts that imitate casual users) and to the behavioural items.
We also report the cosine of $\vd_l$ with an evaluation-awareness direction built from the 16 ``real user'' vs.\ ``evaluator'' pairs of \citet{hua2025steering} and with a refusal direction (\jbb harmful minus benign means, following \citealt{arditi2024refusal}).
To ask whether provenance is present beyond style, we train an in-domain five-fold probe on \polistance and compare it with length and TF-IDF classifiers.

\para{Steering on fixed text.}
We hold the text exactly fixed at \vH from rewriter A for all 950 items and add $\alpha\,\vd_l$ at every position in eight consecutive middle layers (hidden states 9--16 for \llama, 7--14 for \qwen).
We sweep $\alpha \in \{\pm 0.25, \pm 0.5, \pm 1\}$; positive $\alpha$ pushes toward ``LLM-written''.
$\alpha=0.25$ moves the read-out projection by about the natural \vH$\to$\vS difference (1.35 vs.\ 1.63 for \llama at layer 20), and $\alpha=2$ produced incoherent text in calibration.
Controls are norm-matched random directions (an independent Gaussian vector per layer rescaled to $\|\vd_l\|$; three seeds for \llama, two for \qwen, at $\alpha=0.5$ and $1$) and the unsteered model.
Manipulation checks are the read-out projection four layers above the steering band and the verbal self-report.
Degradation checks are neutral \triviaqa accuracy, response length and the share of all-lowercase replies.

\para{Reproducibility.}
We fix seed 42 and use deterministic decoding throughout, and cache every API call so all runs replay offline.
The study used about \$30 of API credit and 3.5 GPU-hours on one RTX A6000 (48\,GB).
