## A. Papers named in the research specification (every page read; notes in `notes/<arxiv_id>.md`)

1. [LLM Evaluators Recognize and Favor Their Own Generations](2404.13076_panickssery_llm_evaluators_recognize_favor_own.pdf)
   - Authors: Arjun Panickssery, Samuel R. Bowman, Shi Feng
   - Year: 2024 · Venue (per Semantic Scholar): Neural Information Processing Systems · Citations: 846
   - arXiv: 2404.13076
   - Why relevant: LLM judges recognise and prefer their own summaries; fine-tuning for self-recognition raises self-preference. Text is the judged item, not the prompt; causal evidence is correlational.

2. [AI–AI bias: Large language models favor communications generated by large language models](2407.12856_laurito_ai_ai_bias.pdf)
   - Authors: Walter Laurito, Benjamin Davis, Peli Grietzer et al.
   - Year: 2024 · Venue (per Semantic Scholar): Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America · Citations: 48
   - arXiv: 2407.12856
   - Why relevant: LLMs choosing between human- and LLM-written item descriptions pick the LLM text far more often than humans do. Establishes AI-AI bias in choice tasks; no mechanism, no internals.

3. [The Hawthorne Effect in Reasoning Models: Evaluating and Steering Test Awareness](2505.14617_abdelnabi_hawthorne_effect_test_awareness.pdf)
   - Authors: Sahar Abdelnabi, Ahmed Salem
   - Year: 2025 · Venue (per Semantic Scholar): Neural Information Processing Systems · Citations: 28
   - arXiv: 2505.14617
   - Why relevant: Linear probe + weight-edit steering of test awareness in 32B reasoning models; steering changes harmful tool-call compliance in both directions (model-dependent sign). Template for 'representation -> safety behaviour' causal claims.

4. [Large Language Models Often Know When They Are Being Evaluated](2505.23836_needham_llms_know_when_being_evaluated.pdf)
   - Authors: J. Needham, Giles Edkins, Govind Pimpale et al.
   - Year: 2025 · Venue (per Semantic Scholar): arXiv.org · Citations: 115
   - arXiv: 2505.23836
   - Why relevant: Frontier models classify transcripts as evaluation vs deployment (best AUC 0.834; humans 0.921). Black-box only; supplies the 'probe question' format we adapt to ask 'was this written by a human or an AI?'. Models cite formality/structure as cues but never LLM authorship.

5. [Probing and Steering Evaluation Awareness of Language Models](2507.01786_nguyen_probing_steering_eval_awareness.pdf)
   - Authors: Jord Nguyen, Khiem Hoang, Carlo Leonardo Attubato et al.
   - Year: 2025 · Venue (per Semantic Scholar): arXiv.org · Citations: 31
   - arXiv: 2507.01786
   - Why relevant: Mean-difference probes for evaluation awareness in Llama-3.3-70B (best layers 23-24, AUROC 0.83 on SAD); the direction tracks length/structure and had almost no causal effect on sandbagging. Recipe and cautionary tale.

6. [RepreGuard: Detecting LLM-Generated Text by Revealing Hidden Representation Patterns](2508.13152_chen_repreguard.pdf)
   - Authors: Xin Chen, Junchao Wu, Shu Yang et al.
   - Year: 2025 · Venue (per Semantic Scholar): Transactions of the Association for Computational Linguistics · Citations: 26
   - arXiv: 2508.13152
   - Why relevant: Unsupervised PCA direction on LLM-minus-human activation differences detects LLM text (avg AUROC 94.9; 84.2 at 64 tokens). Alternative probe recipe; code cloned.

7. [AI Self-preferencing in Algorithmic Hiring: Empirical Evidence and Insights](2509.00462_xu_ai_self_preferencing_hiring.pdf)
   - Authors: Jiannan Xu, Gujie Li, J. Jiang
   - Year: 2025 · Venue (per Semantic Scholar): Conference on Equity and Access in Algorithms, Mechanisms, and Optimization · Citations: 13
   - arXiv: 2509.00462
   - Why relevant: LLM resume screeners prefer LLM-written summaries over human-written ones (67-82% bias in large models, 47-69% even under content-preserving revision); an instruction not to consider authorship cuts the bias. Shows provenance/style effects survive content control.

8. [Templated or fully synthetic? Prompt construction as a confound in measuring LLM political stance beyond writing assistance](2608.11008_chalkidis_templated_or_synthetic_prompt_confound.pdf)
   - Authors: Ilias Chalkidis
   - Year: 2026 · Venue (per Semantic Scholar): arXiv · Citations: 0
   - arXiv: 2608.11008
   - Why relevant: Closest prior work: templated vs LLM-generated evaluation prompts give different measured political stance and refusal (5% vs 2%), attributed to stance-leaking fillers - content, not provenance. Explicitly leaves 'does recognition change behaviour / internal states' open.

9. [Evaluation Awareness in Language Models: Representation, Verbalization, and Control](2608.21766_heidari_eval_awareness_representation_verbalization_control.pdf)
   - Authors: Farzaneh Heidari, Amin Memarian, Guillaume Rabusseau
   - Year: 2026 · Venue (per Semantic Scholar): arXiv · Citations: 2
   - arXiv: 2608.21766
   - Why relevant: Across 6 open models, an eval-awareness direction classifies MASK vs WildChat (AUROC > 0.7 at best layer) but barely predicts verbalised awareness (|rho| < 0.19); steering raises verbalisation. No behavioural outcome.

10. [Linear Probing Provides Robust and Efficient Detection of Machine-Generated Text](2608.24780_quaremba_linear_probing_mgt_detection.pdf)
   - Authors: Gerrit Quaremba, Hanqi Yan, E. Black et al.
   - Year: 2026 · Venue (per Semantic Scholar): arXiv · Citations: 0
   - arXiv: 2608.24780
   - Why relevant: Simple last-token logistic-regression probes on Llama-3-8B detect machine-generated text (in-domain AUC 0.90-1.00, ~100 examples suffice, AUC 0.82-0.85 on 25-50-character texts). The recipe for our 'written by an LLM' probe.
