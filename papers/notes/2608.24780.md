# Linear Probing Provides Robust and Efficient Detection of Machine-Generated Text

- **Authors:** Gerrit Quaremba, Hanqi Yan, Elizabeth Black (King's College London), Denny Vrandečić (Wikimedia Foundation), Elena Simperl (King's College London)
- **Venue/year:** arXiv preprint, v1 dated 25 Aug 2026, cs.CL (no conference venue stated)
- **arXiv id:** 2608.24780
- **Read status:** all 8 chunks (24 pages incl. appendices A-D) read in full.

## Research question and main claims

Questions (quoted): "Are machine- and human-written text linearly separable in latent space, and if so, why? How can we exploit this structure for enhanced MGT detection?"

Claims:
1. Human-written text (HWT) and machine-generated text (MGT) hidden states are linearly separable in a low-dimensional subspace from early layers (as early as layer 6 of Llama-8B, by 2-D PCA visualisation, Fig. 1).
2. Explanation offered: MGT representations are "more compressed, more anisotropic, and lower-dimensional" (lower entropy, effective rank, intrinsic dimension; higher anisotropy) than HWT (Fig. 3, Fig. 9).
3. Simple logistic-regression probes on frozen LM hidden states beat 16 detector baselines in-domain (gains +0.04 to +18.85 AUC points) and out-of-domain (abstract: "+11 AUC"; text: "0.39-11.37 AUC" / "up to 11.83"; Table 2 max delta is +11.89 -- the paper is internally inconsistent on this headline number).
4. Probes need "only 10-100 training samples" to reach near-peak AUC.
5. Probe vectors trained on different datasets are highly aligned within a benchmark and moderately aligned across benchmarks -> "shared latent MGT direction".
6. Projection onto the probe direction correlates with the degree of AI editing (continuous "machineness").

## Methodology (reproducible detail)

### Backbone model
- Probes use "Llama-3-8B (Grattafiori et al., 2024)" (called `Llama-8B`/`Llama-3-8B`); hidden size 4096; figures show layers 0-32. Whether this is the base or Instruct checkpoint for the probes is **not stated** (the Revise *baseline* uses `https://huggingface.co/meta-llama/Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct`). Chat template usage: not stated (inference of mine: raw text is fed, since these are documents not chat turns).
- Ablation backbones (Table 7/9): Llama-3B, Llama-1B, Qwen-32B, Qwen-8B, Qwen-4B, Qwen-0.6B (Qwen3, Yang et al. 2025). Exact HF ids not stated.

### Probe recipe (Section 4) -- THE REUSABLE PART
- Input text x_i is passed through the frozen LM; extract the **last-token** hidden state h_i^(l) at **every layer** l = 1..L (residual stream activations).
- **PCA per layer:** fit PCA on the training activations of that layer, keep top **100** principal components (100/4096 ~= 2.4% of dims). (In the sample-efficiency experiment the PCA is still fit on the full training set; Fig. 12 repeats without PCA.)
- **Classifier:** sklearn logistic regression, L2 penalty, **C = 1** (binary cross-entropy + lambda * ||w||^2), label y=1 for machine-generated.
- **Probe vector:** v^(l) = w^(l) / ||w^(l)||_2.
- **Scoring at inference:** s^(l) = h_test^(l)T v^(l) (projection onto the direction, not the sigmoid probability).
- **LLP (Layer-Averaged Linear Probe):** one probe per layer; final score = mean over layers of the per-layer projection scores, s_LLP = (1/L) sum_l s^(l).
- **CLP (Concatenated-Layer Linear Probe):** concatenate the 100-d PCA features of all L layers (R^{L x 100}) and train one logistic regression; score analogously.
- Not difference-in-means, not PCA-of-differences: explicitly supervised regularised logistic regression. Authors attribute their gain over RepreGuard "primarily to supervised, regularized learning, which suppresses noisy dimensions".
- Compute: "1-3 minutes on an A100 (40GB)" per probe variant including activation extraction and inference.

### Training-set sizes
- Per subset: **1,500 training and 500 test instances, balanced across labels**, randomly sampled (16 subsets total).
- Sample-efficiency sweep (Fig. 5): shares 0.7%, 3.3%, 6.7%, 16.7%, 33.3% of the 1,500 (i.e. ~10, 50, 100, 250, 500 -- my arithmetic; paper says "3.3-6.7% of the training data (10-100 samples)"), repeated over random seeds (number of seeds not stated).

### Linearity test (Section 3.1)
- MLP probes of depth 1-5 on mid-layer representations (M4GT subsets ArXiv, PeerRead, Reddit, Wikipedia). AUC decreases monotonically with depth, ID and OOD (Fig. 2; e.g. ArXiv ID roughly 0.84 at depth 1 to ~0.75 at depth 5, Wikipedia ~0.96 -> ~0.925; read off a bar chart, no table). MLP width/training details not stated.

### Representation-quality metrics (Section 3.2, App. A.1)
- Entropy (von Neumann, alpha=1, of normalised Gram-matrix eigenvalues), Effective Rank (exp of entropy of normalised singular values), Anisotropy (sigma_1^2 / sum sigma_i^2 on centred activations), Intrinsic Dimension (TwoNN estimator, `scikit-dimension`). Computed per layer on M4GT Wikipedia human vs machine activations with Llama-8B (Fig. 3); extended in Fig. 9 to arxiv/reddit/peerread and selected DetectRL/RAID/MultiSocial/TSM subsets. Result: MGT has lower entropy (roughly 1-1.5 vs 3.5-4 on Wikipedia), lower effective rank, lower intrinsic dimension, higher anisotropy in middle/later layers. Authors note differences are "sometimes less pronounced" on datasets with adversarial attacks or multiple generators.

### Benchmarks (App. B.1)
- **M4GT** (Wang et al. 2024) -- Wikipedia/Reddit/PeerRead/ArXiv subsets; generators shown in Fig. 8 include bloomz, cohere, dolly, gpt4. Used only for the representation analysis (detection results saturated).
- **DetectRL** (Wu et al. 2024), Task 2 domain dataset: ArXiv, Reddit, Yelp, News (XSum); each split mixes several generator LLMs and attacks (prompt-based, paraphrase, perturbation, mixing). https://github.com/NLP2CT/DetectRL
- **MultiSocial** (Macko et al. 2025): social-media text, 22 languages, 5 platforms; subsets en/de/ru/zh. https://github.com/kinit-sk/multisocial
- **RAID** (Dugan et al. 2024): >10M samples; generator subsets Cohere/GPT4/Llama/Mistral, mixed domains/decoding/attacks. https://github.com/liamdugan/raid
- **TSM** (Quaremba et al. 2026): Wikipedia editing tasks -- First Paragraph (FP), Paragraph Extension (PE, mixed human+machine text), Summarisation (SUM), Text Style Transfer (TST, short texts with minor stylistic edits); English split, six generators. https://github.com/gerritq/tsm_bench
- **APT-Eval** (Saha & Feizi 2025): 15k AI-polished texts; 200 instances drawn from each of 4 polish levels (extremely minor, minor, slightly major, major). https://github.com/ShoumikSaha/ai-polished-text
- **EditLens** (Thai et al. 2026): AI-edited human text in 4 domains, 3 editing LLMs. https://github.com/pangramlabs/EditLens

## Baselines and metrics
- Metric: **AUC (AUROC)** throughout; ECE for calibration; accuracy/F1 for the 3-way EditLens task.
- 16 baselines, implemented with Llama-3-8B where applicable: zero-shot -- Likelihood, LLR, Rank, GECScore, Revise, RAIDAR, FastDetectGPT, Binoculars; supervised -- EditLens (released RoBERTa-Large), ID (PHD intrinsic dimension + LR, XLM-RoBERTa backbone), OpenAI-RoBERTa, RADAR (Vicuna-7B), RepreGuard, BiScope, TextFluoroscopy (backbone swapped to Llama-3-8B), fully fine-tuned RoBERTa-base (XLM-RoBERTa-base for MultiSocial; batch 32, lr 2e-5, weight decay 0.01, 2 epochs).

## Controls for confounds
- **Length:** only an ablation where train+test are truncated to n characters (Table 4). No matching of length between human and machine classes, and no test that the direction is not partly a length/fluency direction.
- **Content/topic:** not controlled. Human and machine texts in these benchmarks share a domain/prompt source but are not content-matched pairs (except TSM/APT-Eval/EditLens edit tasks, where the machine text is an edit of human text; the PE/TST/SUM tasks are thus closer to content-controlled, and those are exactly where AUC drops to 0.81-0.95 OOD).
- **Style/formatting:** not controlled; authors acknowledge CLP "overfitting to stylistic cues associated with formal writing" on News.
- **PCA artefact:** controlled (no-PCA variants nearly identical).
- **Nonlinearity:** tested with MLP depth sweep.
- No causal test (no steering/ablation of the direction). The authors state the results "do not establish the existence of a universal latent MGT direction" and that "stability, causality, and universality" are future work.
- Adequacy (my assessment): adequate for a detection paper; **not adequate** to claim the model "represents authorship" as opposed to representing correlated surface properties (fluency, formality, low lexical diversity, absence of typos).

## Limitations
Stated by authors: only four representation metrics; model scaling not systematically studied; only logistic-regression probes; no proof of a *universal* direction and confounds possible; AI-edit detection not benchmarked against dedicated methods; requires white-box access.

Noticed by me:
- All texts are **documents/passages** (abstracts, reviews, social posts, Wikipedia paragraphs), not *prompts/requests addressed to the model*. Nothing is said about user-turn text inside a chat template.
- The reading model is a detector reading text passively; there is no link from the direction to any downstream *behaviour* of the model.
- Cross-benchmark AUC transfer not reported; cosine similarity across benchmarks is only "moderate".
- Headline OOD gain number inconsistent (+11 / 11.37 / 11.83 / 11.89).
- Base vs Instruct checkpoint and tokenisation/BOS/chat-template details not stated; number of seeds not stated; "ID" baseline is attributed once to Hu et al. 2023 (apparently a citation slip for Tulchinskii et al. 2023).
- Short-text performance is tested only on ArXiv abstracts truncated to n characters; real short prompts (one-sentence requests) are a different distribution.

## Relevance to our project

What we can reuse:
- **Probe recipe for an "LLM-written" direction:** last-token residual activation at every layer -> (optional) per-layer PCA to 100 dims -> sklearn LogisticRegression(L2, C=1) -> unit-normalise weight -> score = projection; average projections across layers (LLP). No-PCA is as good or slightly better, so for steering we can train directly in the full 4096-d space (needed anyway to get a vector we can add to the residual stream). ~100 labelled examples suffice; 1,500 is what they used.
- **Design choices validated by ablation:** last-token > mean-pooling (up to -0.155 AUC for mean pooling); mid layers (their layer 16 of 32 in Llama-3-8B) best for OOD; single first/last layer is worse; regularisation strength irrelevant; small models work (Llama-1B loses <= 0.034, Qwen-0.6B <= 0.036), Qwen3 slightly better than Llama-3.
- **Short-text caveat with numbers:** AUC 0.82-0.85 at 25-50 characters, ~0.97-0.98 at 125 characters. Typical user prompts are short, so expect weaker separability for short prompts and report AUC by length bin.
- **Datasets** for pre-training/validating a generic human-vs-machine direction before testing it on prompts: DetectRL, RAID, MultiSocial, TSM (TST and PE subsets are the closest to "same content, different author").
- **Cosine-similarity-of-probe-vectors** analysis (Fig. 4) as a template for asking: is the direction learned on documents the same as one learned on human-vs-LLM *prompts*?
- **Continuous "machineness"** result suggests a dose-response design: lightly vs heavily LLM-rewritten prompts should move monotonically along the direction (r ~0.6-0.7 with edit distance here).

Implications for our design:
- A linear human-vs-LLM direction very likely exists in any open-weight model we use and is cheap to obtain, but this paper gives **no evidence that it is causal for behaviour** and no evidence it is about *authorship* rather than surface style. Our contribution must add (a) content-controlled pairs (same request in human vs LLM style), (b) controls/regressions for length, politeness, clarity, formatting, and (c) causal interventions (activation steering along the direction, or projecting it out) with behavioural read-outs (refusal, sycophancy, compliance, accuracy).
- Because probes here read a passage's last token, for chat prompts we must decide the token position (last token of the user turn vs the post-template assistant-start token); the paper does not address this, so we should compare both.
- Their OOD drop on edit-style tasks (PE 0.81, TST 0.86) signals that when content is held fixed the signal weakens -- relevant to our paraphrase-controlled stimuli.

Gap left open: nothing on prompts/instructions, nothing on chat-formatted inputs, nothing on whether the responding model's behaviour depends on this direction, and no content-matched control. Whether the model "knows the prompter is an LLM" in a behaviourally relevant sense is untouched.
