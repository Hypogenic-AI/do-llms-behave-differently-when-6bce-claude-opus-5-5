- **Authors:** Xin Chen*, Junchao Wu*, Shu Yang, Runzhe Zhan, Zeyu Wu, Ziyang Luo, Di Wang, Min Yang, Lidia S. Chao, Derek F. Wong (NLP2CT Lab, University of Macau; SIAT-CAS; KAUST; HKBU)

- **arXiv id:** 2508.13152
- **Read status:** all 7 chunks (19 pages incl. appendix A.1-A.5) read in full.

## Research question and main claims

Hypothesis (quoted): "LLMs exhibit distinct hidden representation patterns when processing LGT compared to HWT, due to their different perceptions of statistical patterns in these text types." (LGT = LLM-generated text, HWT = human-written text.)

Claims:
1. A surrogate ("observer") LLM's hidden activations differ systematically between LGT and HWT: similar for roughly tokens 0-20, diverging after ~20 tokens, with LGT showing "a consistently higher overall activation level"; lower layers (~1-11) similar, layers 11-32 differ more (Fig. 1, Llama-3.1-8B, 1000 pairs from 4 LLMs).
2. A single per-layer direction obtained by PCA on LGT-minus-HWT activation differences, plus a threshold, gives a detector ("RepreGuard") with average 94.92 +/- 0.70% AUROC and 82.44 +/- 1.84% TPR@0.01 over ID+OOD settings, beating RoBERTa-classifier and Binoculars (by "11.05% and 05.88% AUROC" on average).
3. Works few-shot (16 pairs), transfers across generators and domains, is robust to paraphrase/perturbation attacks, text length and sampling strategy.

## Methodology (reproducible detail)

### Direction-extraction recipe (Section 3.2) -- THE REUSABLE PART
1. **Training set:** N *pairs* (T_LGT^i, T_HWT^i). Main experiments: **512 pairs** (also 16/32/64/128/256/1024 in the shots ablation; 250 pairs per LLM in the token-ratio ablation).
2. **Surrogate model M** ("observer"): default **Llama-3.1-8B** (base, non-instruct; https://huggingface.co/meta-llama/Llama-3.1-8B). Text is simply passed through the model (no prompt template described).
3. **Activations:** for each token t_j and each layer l collect hidden state h_j^l in R^d. Activations are captured "from the end to the beginning of the token at all layers"; only a fraction of tokens counted from the end is used -- the **activation token ratio**; best = **0.1** (the last 10% of tokens; Appendix A.1 calls "Activation Tokens (the last 10% of tokens)"). Performance is flat from 0.1 to ~0.6 then drops sharply toward 1.0 (Fig. 4).
4. **Pairwise differences:** Delta A_i^l = h_LGT^{l,i} - h_HWT^{l,i} for each pair i and layer l. How token positions of two texts of different lengths are aligned/pooled before subtraction is **not stated** in the paper (Quaremba et al. describe RepreGuard as using "mean-pooled token representations").
5. **PCA per layer:** P_l = PCA(Delta A^l); P_l is "the probing vector that differentiates between LGT and HWT at layer l". The paper does not say explicitly "first principal component" (Quaremba et al. describe it as the first PC of the activation-difference matrix; this follows Zou et al. 2023 Representation Engineering, which the paper says it follows). No learned classifier.
6. **Score:** per-token RepreScore(t_j) = (1/|L|) sum_l h_l(t_j) . P_l (mean over **all layers** of the projection); text score = mean over tokens: RepreScore(T) = (1/n) sum_j RepreScore(t_j). (I.e. mean-pooling of projections, in contrast to last-token in Quaremba et al.)
7. **Threshold:** theta = argmax_theta' (TPR(theta') + (1 - FPR(theta'))) on the *training* data (Youden's J). Classify as LGT if RepreScore > theta.
- Layer selection: all layers averaged; no single-layer ablation reported.
- Observed score ranges (Fig. 3, direction modelled on Multi-LLM data): HWT RepreScore mostly in [-2, 2], LGT in [0, 8]; distribution overlap ChatGPT 0.98%, Llama-2-70b 1.27%, Google-PaLM 17.39%, Claude-instant 14.81%.

### Data
- **DetectRL** benchmark (Wu et al. 2024): 4 domains -- academic (ArXiv abstracts, https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/spsayakpaul/arxiv-paper-abstracts/data), news (XSum), creative (Writing Prompts), social (Yelp Review). Per domain 2,800 LGT/HWT pairs; generators **ChatGPT, Claude-instant, Google-PaLM, Llama-2-70B** (https://huggingface.co/meta-llama/Llama-2-70b-chat-hf).
- Bootstrapped 5 times per LLM: train = 512 pairs, test = 1,000 pairs each iteration. "Multi-LLMs" = the four LLMs' data combined, sampled equally.
- **RAID** (Dugan et al. 2024) for decoding-strategy robustness (chat models: llama-chat, mistral-chat, mpt-chat; non-chat: mistral, mpt, gpt2; greedy vs sampling; with/without repetition penalty).
- **Reddit 2025** human-only data for memorisation check: https://huggingface.co/datasets/tensorshield/reddit_dataset_157 (2,000 samples, written after Llama-3.1-8B's cutoff).
- Attacks: DIPPER paraphraser (Krishna et al. 2023); TextBugger perturbation (Li et al. 2019).
- LGT-pretraining experiment: surrogate further pre-trained on 1 million LGT texts (details of that corpus not stated).

## Baselines and metrics
- Baselines: RoBERTa-base fine-tuned classifier; LRR (GPT-Neo-2.7B scoring); DetectGPT (T5-small perturbation, GPT-Neo-2.7B scoring); Fast-DetectGPT (GPT-Neo-2.7B scoring, GPT-J-6B sampling); "Straight-forward Detector" (Str-Detect.: directly asking an LLM whether text is HWT or LGT; prompt and model not given); Binoculars (Falcon-7B + Falcon-7B-Instruct).
- Metrics: **AUROC** and **TPR@0.01** -- described as "a standard FPR threshold of 0.01% TPR (TPR@0.01)" following Hans et al. (2024) (i.e. TPR at 0.01% FPR as written; ambiguous whether 0.01 or 0.01%).
- Thresholds for all detectors are set on training data, not on the test set (their "strict" OOD setting).
- Statistics: 5 bootstrap rounds, mean +/- standard deviation reported; no significance tests.

## Controls for confounds
- **Length:** train/test evaluated at fixed sizes 64/128/192/256 (Fig. 6). Human and LGT texts are paired (DetectRL pairs share the source prompt/topic) but length matching between the two members of a pair is not described.
- **Content:** DetectRL pairs are human text and LLM text for the same source item (e.g. same abstract title / review prompt), so there is topic pairing, and the difference-of-pairs construction partially removes topic. Not a strict paraphrase control. The DIPPER paraphrase experiment keeps content and changes wording, and the direction still separates.
- **Lexical confound:** A.1 shows same tokens score differently in the two classes (context- rather than token-driven).
- **Memorisation:** A.2.
- **Style/formatting/fluency/politeness:** not controlled. The paper itself frames what is detected as "statistical patterns".
- No causal intervention (no steering/ablation); no test of whether the direction affects model behaviour.
- Adequacy (my assessment): sufficient for a detector; the claim that the model has an "intrinsic understanding of the differences" is not tested beyond linear readout.

## Limitations
Stated by the authors: no dedicated limitations section. They note surrogate performance "may relate to their architecture and training data, requiring proper evaluation"; TPR@0.01 falls when the surrogate is pre-trained on LGT; AUROC on 64-token texts with ChatGPT training is slightly below other detectors.

Noticed by me:
- Alignment of token positions when subtracting paired activations, the number of principal components, sign convention of P_l, and any normalisation/centering are not specified in the text (must consult the code).
- "TPR@0.01" is defined ambiguously (0.01 vs 0.01% FPR).
- In independent re-evaluation (Quaremba et al., arXiv 2608.24780, Llama-3-8B, 1,500 training texts) RepreGuard is much weaker: in-domain AUC 0.50-0.98 and OOD frequently ~0.50 (e.g. DetectRL ArXiv OOD 0.5327; TSM 0.49-0.58), i.e. the unsupervised PCA direction with mean pooling transfers poorly outside the generator-transfer setting used here. Domain transfer here is leave-one-out with three training domains, a much easier setting than single-domain transfer.
- All generators are 2023-era (ChatGPT, Claude-instant, PaLM, Llama-2-70B); no modern models.
- Texts are documents (abstracts, news, stories, reviews) of >= 64 tokens; no prompts/questions/instructions and no chat-formatted input. The finding that the first ~20 tokens look alike implies weak signal for short prompts.
- Mean activation "level" difference (Fig. 1) could reflect generic properties such as predictability/perplexity of the text to the observer rather than an authorship concept.
- Str-Detect baseline prompt is not provided.

## Relevance to our project

What we can reuse:
- **Unsupervised paired-difference recipe** (RepE-style): for N content-matched pairs (LLM-style prompt, human-style prompt), compute per-layer activation differences, take the leading PCA component as the "LLM-written" direction, score new inputs by projection (averaged across layers), threshold by Youden's J on training data. This is exactly suited to our stimuli because our pairs are content-controlled by construction, so the pair difference isolates style/author.
- **Data budget:** 16 pairs already give ~90 AUROC here; 512 pairs is their default. A few hundred prompt pairs should suffice.
- **Token position guidance:** signal is concentrated at the *end* of the text (last 10% of tokens best; using all tokens hurts), and the first ~20 tokens carry little -- supports reading the last token(s) of the user turn; consistent with Quaremba et al.'s last-token > mean-pooling result.
- **Model choice evidence:** Llama-3.1-8B (base) best; Phi-2 and Gemma-2B(-Instruct) are strong small alternatives; Gemma-7B, Falcon-7B, GPT-Neo poor. Instruct variants generally work but Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct is slightly below base (93.59 vs 94.74 AUROC; TPR 74.66 vs 80.92). We need instruct/chat models for behavioural measures, so expect a modest hit.
- **Metrics:** AUROC and TPR at low FPR; 5x bootstrap with mean ± SD.
- **Datasets:** DetectRL pairs (four generators x four domains) as an independent corpus to derive a generic human-vs-LLM direction and then test whether it fires on our LLM-style prompts (transfer test from documents to prompts).

Implications for our design:
- Two competing recipes exist (unsupervised PCA-of-differences with mean pooling here; supervised logistic regression on last token in Quaremba et al.), and the latter beat the former substantially in independent comparison. We should compute both (plus plain difference-in-means, which Quaremba et al. note is "closely related and often identical") and report agreement (cosine similarity) between them.
- Short inputs are the weak point (AUROC 84.22 at 64 tokens; nothing shorter tested). Our prompts will often be < 64 tokens: stratify by length and consider length-matched pairs.
- The direction here is interpreted only as a detector feature. Our added value is the causal step: add/subtract the direction during generation and measure refusal / sycophancy / compliance / accuracy, with surface-feature controls.

Gap left open: no prompts, no chat setting, no behavioural consequence, no steering, no disentangling of "LLM authorship" from fluency/formality/length; nothing about whether a model *responds* differently to LLM-written input.
